\begin{enumerate}[label=(\arabic*)]
  \item \textbf{Relations are nodes that hold equations, rules and aggregations.} Graph-based retrieval systems store simple triples that a model extracts from the text. Financial ontologies hold classes and properties but no arithmetic, and the XBRL calculation linkbase holds only sums. The closest design, Wolfram's \code{FormulaData}~\citep{wolfram_formuladata}, is a library of formulas solvable for any variable, but it is fixed, does not cover finance and is not tested against data. In our concept graph a relation is itself a node that joins several quantities, and it can hold one of three things: an equation that can be solved for any of its quantities, a rule that states how quantities affect each other, or an aggregation that records which lines add up to a subtotal. Equations and aggregations are admitted only when real filings confirm them. We found no earlier graph of this kind.

  \item \textbf{The formula comes from a verified graph, not from the model.} In program-of-thought systems the model writes the calculation itself, and it is executed without being checked. Two recent works take the formula out of the model: FinanceReasoning retrieves Python functions by similarity, but they are unverified and no document is read; VeriFin checks another system's answer against XBRL facts, so it covers only tagged quantities and works only after an answer exists. Our system takes the formula from the graph, where it has already been verified across filings, and computes the answer from the text of the document.

  \item \textbf{Documents are read at question time, and every number is checked against the page.} Existing knowledge-graph systems turn a document into facts once, before any question is seen, so a mistake made then cannot be corrected later. Our system stores the text and tables whole and reads them when a question arrives. Before a number is used, code checks that the model's quote is printed in the cited passage and takes the value, sign and unit from the page, not from the model. PageIndex~\citep{vectify2026mafin}, the strongest published system on FinanceBench, also reasons over the document's layout, but its formula is the model's and no citation is checked.

  \item \textbf{Where to look is measured from data.} FinGEAR, HiREC and HC-RAG route a question to a part of a 10-K using a word list or a tree of the document. None estimates from data how likely a quantity is to be printed in a given part of a filing, or uses that likelihood to decide whether to search for a value or derive it. Our bridge does both, from counts over 32,288 filings. The chunk ranker is trained on the filings' own inline-XBRL tags, which have been used before to label numbers (FiNER~\citep{loukas2022finer}) but not to train a retriever.
\end{enumerate}
