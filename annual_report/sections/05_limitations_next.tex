\section{Where it still goes wrong, and what comes next}
\label{sec:next}

\subsection{What the results do and do not show}

They show that a graph of verified equations, used as a prior over text read at question time with every citation checked, answers 88\% of FinanceBench's numeric questions and 78\% overall with four refusals; that the solver answers every textbook question once naming is right; and that each knowledge base produced a measured gain when it went in. They do not yet show the claim at its strongest: FinanceBench names its company and period and mostly needs one or two cells, so the questions the graph is \emph{for}, a quantity several equations from anything printed or spread over several filings, are under-represented, and the independent question set the project's working agreements call for is not yet written. Text answers are judged by reading, by us, against golds that are sometimes arguable, and every comparison with another system is across different graders.

\subsection{The defects, from the last two runs}

Every miss in the last FinanceBench and Multi-Doc runs was traced; they fall into six kinds, none of them retrieval.

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Fixed caps instead of a value rule.} The loop stops on task, depth, route and round caps. General Mills' FY2018 inventory was ``derived'' from 2 of a subtotal's 10 parts because the task cap stopped the search; the aggregation rule has no coverage floor, a gap known since September.
\item \textbf{The cited figure is not the target.} Grounding checks that a quote is printed, not that it is the thing asked: a margin row was accepted as gross profit (then divided by revenue to give $\approx 0$), a wrong line or a wrong year's column was cited four times. A period check against the column header and a kind-of-number check are the obvious guards; the second exists and must be made stricter.
\item \textbf{Units on computed figures.} A figure the answer stage computes in code carries no unit, so the evaluator treats it as base units and scales it wrongly (``\$2 billion in millions'' $\to$ 0.002; a percentage multiplied by 100 twice). Four Multi-Doc numeric misses.
\item \textbf{The question's or the filing's definition must beat the graph's formula.} Nike's inventory turnover is defined in the question over \emph{average} inventory; the graph's ending-inventory identity was used. Eli Lilly's own dollar ``gross margin'' lost to the textbook ratio. And one route recorded a derived net profit as a margin (the identity read the wrong way), so one of Best Buy's three years was zero.
\item \textbf{Shape.} The evaluator collapses a multi-figure text answer to one number when the question's wording looks numeric (seven Multi-Doc cases, right in substance).
\item \textbf{Judgement and reading.} Of 17 wrong FinanceBench text answers, 6 are a verdict against the gold on a judgement call (``is capital intensity high''), 4 a ``cannot determine'' where the gold has a value, 1 was answered from the wrong company's filing. The four refusals are a figure in an 8-K's exhibit, a passage read but judged not to answer, and a quantity found in a note while the question asked for the income-statement line.
\end{enumerate}

Beneath these sit known limits of the knowledge bases: the bridge's 20-F rows over-predict by half because 2025 foreign filers tag IFRS while the measured rows are mostly US GAAP (a filer-class split is needed); only one quarter of the Notes data is in and releases and 8-Ks carry no tags; reviewers noted wrong bindings and near-duplicate entities in the textbook graph beyond the repaired set; and three thresholds (the 20\% search-or-derive line, the 1\% bridge floor, the 0.4 meaning floor in document search) are placeholders not yet measured.

\subsection{Plan}

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{The value rule} replaces the caps: a worth per target passed down the formula tree and one queue of open actions ranked by worth $\times$ chance $\div$ cost, where chance is the calibrated bridge share times the calibrated retrieval mass times the reader's measured rate. The three numbers are in hand. With it, the coverage floor on aggregations, and the question's own definition taking precedence over the graph's.
\item \textbf{Target checks on a citation}: the column's period and the kind of number, so that a printed figure must also be the asked one.
\item \textbf{Units carried through every computed figure}, and the evaluator keeping a text answer's shape when it holds several figures.
\item \textbf{The independent question set}: questions written from real filings that need derivation through several equations, several documents (a 10-K and the following 10-Q, a parent and its co-registrant) and that the documents cannot answer, so the method is measured on what it is for.
\item \textbf{Benchmarks continued}: the full Multi-Doc-2025 test split, DocFinQA's human-validated test split~\citep{reddy2024docfinqa}, FinanceBench after each change; the bridge's filer-class split and more Notes quarters; graph repairs by authored decisions.
\item \textbf{Writing up} the three contributions: equations as verified, solvable nodes; the commitment measurement and the graph-as-prior design; the data arrangement for learned pieces (fit on filings that label themselves, evaluate on benchmarks whose companies never trained anything).
\end{enumerate}

The project was budgeted at six to eight weeks of the thesis's time and has taken more, because each measurement opened a tractable next step. What it has produced, a verified equation graph over the SEC vocabulary, a graph of document kinds, a measured bridge between them, and a loop that reasons backward from a target through typed quantities with data dependencies, is the substrate the thesis's reasoning layer needs, and a first instance of its subject.
